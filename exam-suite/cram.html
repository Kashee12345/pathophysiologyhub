pHCO₂HCO₃Interpretation
↓↑nRespiratory acidosis, uncompensated (acute)
↓↑↑Respiratory acidosis, partially compensated
n <7.40↑↑Respiratory acidosis, fully compensated (chronic — COPD)
↑↓nRespiratory alkalosis (anxiety, pain, fever, early PE)
↓n↓Metabolic acidosis (DKA, shock, renal failure, diarrhoea)
↓↓↓Metabolic acidosis, compensating (Kussmaul)
↑n↑Metabolic alkalosis (vomiting, NG suction, diuretics)
ROME — Respiratory Opposite, Metabolic Equal.
The problem is the value moving the same direction as pH; the other is the compensation.
Normals pH 7.35–7.45 · CO₂ 35–45 · HCO₃ 22–26 · PaO₂ 80–100 · SaO₂ >95%
Lungs compensate in minutes; kidneys in hours to days — so full compensation implies a chronic process.
Vomiting loses acid → alkalosis. Diarrhoea loses bicarbonate → acidosis.
Hypoxemia = low O₂ in blood. Hypoxia = low O₂ at tissue. Anemia and CO poisoning cause hypoxia with a normal PaO₂.
